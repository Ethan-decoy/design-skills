# Visual Design

This is an English translation of [SKILL.md](SKILL.md). The Chinese original is the primary version for maintenance and validation.

Use the existing interface, real content, brand assets, and the user's requirements to determine who the software is for, what they most need to see clearly, and how the product should feel. Follow confirmed design conventions. Identify concrete relationships worth borrowing from reference images, such as the proportion of images to text, the rhythm of whitespace, or the areas occupied by different colors. Apply them to this product's content.

## Express the product's character visually

Decide what should lead, what should support it, and in what order people should look. Use area, position, alignment, and light–dark contrast to organize the composition. Establish a rhythm among similar elements and introduce variation where it matters. Precision can come from aligning readouts on a common baseline. Calm can come from an uninterrupted flow of body text and a visually secondary toolbar. When several treatments express the hierarchy clearly, use the audience and product positioning to decide which visual qualities to emphasize. Explain how a consequential choice affects reading, use, or product recognition.

Use the relationships between neighboring elements to convey meaning. Differences between spacing within and between groups can signal what belongs together. Font size, weight, line length, and line height shape the rhythm of reading. Test typography with long titles, numbers, units, or a mix of Chinese and Latin text from the actual content. Use content density to decide where to group elements closely and where to leave space.

Give colors and graphics clear roles. Compare how much space accent colors occupy and how they contrast with the background so people can distinguish branding, selection, and warnings when they appear together. Use text or shapes as additional cues for important states. Crop images to keep the subject visible. Keep the outlines and stroke weights of icons at the same level in harmony with the letterforms. Preserve the data meaning of colors assigned to values or categories in a chart.

## Build, improve, and review

Read the whole request to decide what to deliver. “Build a page for me to look at” calls for a viewable page. “Take a look and fix it” calls for changes to the existing work. When the user wants a design proposal, provide a visual or description they can evaluate. When improving existing work, retain worthwhile visual qualities and prioritize the relationships that most affect the product's character or the recognition of information. When you can render the interface, inspect it with relevant viewport widths, real content, or overlapping states.

Give feedback in the conversation when the user asks only for an evaluation or to discuss possible changes. Identify the specific area, what you see, how it affects the design goal, and which choices are worth retaining or changing. Distinguish departures from confirmed conventions from optional style suggestions. When you have only style code, mark conclusions that depend on rendering as pending.
